himmel guide · remote setup part 4

Two stations, one Telegram group

A station is a computer that runs himmel. This page connects two stations so they can send each other messages through one Telegram group. The two stations can be yours and another person's, or two computers of your own. They do not need to be on the same network: everything goes through Telegram. It takes about 15 minutes.

What you will have at the end

  • Each station has a new Telegram bot of its own, its bridge bot.
  • The two bridge bots share one private Telegram group, and talk to each other there.
  • Each station runs a poller: a small program that keeps checking Telegram for you.
  • The last line of your setup says DONE:.
  • Your main bot, the one you already chat with, stays private and stays out of the group.

Who talks to whom

  • You talk only to your own main bot, in your private chat with it, the same way you do today.
  • The two bridge bots talk to each other in the group. Your station reads what the other bridge bot posts, and posts its answers there.
  • People who post in the group are ignored, you and the other person too. Nobody gets a reply there. Typing your message in the group does not send it to the other station.

Words on this page

Station
A computer that runs himmel.
Operator
The person who runs a station. That is you, or the other person.
Your chief
The main Claude session on your station. It reads what you send to your main bot.
Main bot
The Telegram bot you made in part 1. Only you chat with it. It never joins a group.
Bridge bot
A second, new bot you make on this page. It talks only to the other station's bridge bot.
Token
A long secret password that Telegram gives you for a bot. Whoever has it controls the bot, so paste it only into the helper and never post it.
Username
A Telegram name that starts with @, such as @example_bot.
Group owner
The person who makes the group. We call that side Station A, and the other side Station B.
Supergroup
Telegram's bigger kind of group. Bridge bots need one. You make it in step 2.2.
Admin rights
Extra powers Telegram can give a group member, such as deleting messages. Bridge bots are admins with every power switched off.
Terminal
The app where you type commands. On a Mac: press Cmd+Space, type Terminal, press Enter.
The helper
The setup command you run in the Terminal. It finds every id for you.
Peer
The other station. The helper's command is called peer.
Poller
A small program that keeps checking Telegram for you. The helper starts it.
DONE: and FIX:
The helper's last line. DONE: means it worked. FIX: names one thing to do: do it, then run the same command again.

Four steps

  1. Make a bridge botEach station, in Telegram.
  2. Make the groupThe group owner only, once, in Telegram.
  3. Run one commandEach station, in its Terminal.
  4. CheckEach station: one command that only looks.

Before you start

You need:

  • Part 1 done: your main bot works. If not, do part 1 first. It takes about 5 minutes. Stopped part 1 before pressing Start? Run its command again and press Enter at the token prompt: it keeps the saved token and carries on.
  • The Telegram app on your phone, signed in.
  • A Terminal window of your own. Not Claude, and not a ! command inside Claude. The helper handles secrets, so it refuses to run there.

On a Mac, open Terminal like this: press Cmd+Space, type Terminal, press Enter.

In the Terminal, go to your himmel folder. This page assumes it is ~/himmel:

cd ~/himmel

Is himmel somewhere else? Type cd and a space. Drag the himmel folder from your file manager into the Terminal window. Press Enter. Run every command on this page from that folder.

Watch out for these

  • Your main bot never joins the group. Only the two bridge bots go in.
  • Switch the group to Public, then back to Private. That makes it a supergroup.
  • Switch off "Change group info" and "Pin messages" for members.
  • Make each bridge bot an admin, with every admin right switched off.
  • Add bots by their username. Invite links do not work for bots.
  • Never post anonymously "as the group". The bridge ignores those messages.
  • Run the helper in a plain Terminal, not inside Claude.
  • On a Mac, stop it from sleeping. In System Settings → Battery (or Energy), turn on "Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off". When the Mac sleeps, the poller stops. Telegram keeps waiting messages for only 24 hours.

Which side are you? Pick one to see only your steps. Two computers of your own? You do both sides: pick "All steps".

1. Make your bridge bot

each station

1.1Make a new bot

In Telegram, open a chat with @BotFather. It is Telegram's official bot for making bots, with a blue check mark. Send it this:

send to @BotFather
/newbot

It asks for a name: type any name. Then it asks for a username: type one that ends in bot. This must be a new bot, not your main bot. Two computers of your own? Make one bridge bot for each.

You should see

A reply with the line "Use this token to access the HTTP API". The long line below it is the token. You paste it in step 3.

Closed that chat?

Send /mybots to @BotFather, pick the bot, then press API Token.

1.2Let your bot talk to other bots

Send this to @BotFather. Pick the new bot. Open Bot Settings. Turn Bot-to-Bot mode on.

send to @BotFather

You should see

If not

Check that you picked the new bridge bot, not your main bot.

each station

3.1Run the helper

In your own Terminal (not Claude), in your himmel folder, paste this and press Enter:

The first time, it asks for your bridge bot's token. Paste it and press Enter. Nothing shows on screen while you paste: that is on purpose. Next time, just press Enter there.

It may stop and wait for you to send hello in the group (step 3.2).

Numbered lines that end in ok.

3.2Send hello when it asks

If the helper asks you to send the word hello in the group ("Waiting up to 10 minutes..."), send it now. A hello sent before that is not seen. Station B: you must be in the group first (step 2.B).

Who goes first

  • Two computers of your own: start the command on the first one. While it says "Waiting for the other station's hello", run the same command on the second one. If the first one gave up meanwhile, run it there once more. Nothing breaks.
  • The other station finished first: you still get DONE:. Ask the other operator to run the same command once more. It changes nothing on their side. It just posts their hello again for you.
  • On a Mac: turn on "Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off" (System Settings → Battery, or Energy). A sleeping Mac stops the poller.

4. Check everything

each station

4.1Run the check

This command only looks. It changes nothing, so it is safe to run any time.

bash scripts/remote-setup/remote-setup.sh status
You should see 
DONE: status — all shipped parts ok
If not

The last line starts with FIX: and names the part and the command to run. Run that command.

Why doesn't it answer me?

People never get an answer in the group. The group is only for the two bridge bots. Your bridge bot quietly ignores these, and never tells you it did:

  • every message from a person, including you;
  • a new message that does not start by addressing your bridge bot (a reply inside a conversation the bots already started does go through);
  • edited messages and forwarded messages;
  • messages that arrive too fast (there is a limit per minute);
  • anything from a chat other than the group you set up;
  • posts made anonymously "as the group".

To talk to your own station, message your main bot in its private chat.

Changed a bot or account?

You made a new bridge bot

First, the group owner adds the new bot to the group and makes it an admin with every right off (steps 2.4 and 2.5). Then run this on your station, and paste the new bot's token when it asks:

bash scripts/remote-setup/remote-setup.sh peer --replace

The other station made a new bridge bot

After they have done the card above, run the same command on your station. Press Enter at the token prompt. It finds their new bot, removes the old one from your setup, and asks you to type yes before it removes it.

bash scripts/remote-setup/remote-setup.sh peer --replace

You made a new main bot

Run this for the main bot itself (part 1):

bash scripts/remote-setup/remote-setup.sh bot --replace

The bridge does not follow the new main bot by itself yet, so ask your chief.

You changed your Telegram account, or made a new group

Not automatic yet. Ask your chief.

Undo

To disconnect from the other station and stop the poller:

bash scripts/remote-setup/remote-setup.sh peer --remove

It asks you to type yes first. If it stops half way, run it again: it finishes the job. To delete the bridge bot itself, send /deletebot to @BotFather. To take it out of the group, remove it there.

Optional: let your chiefs hand each other jobs

Not needed for DONE:. It needs a one-time check that you and the other operator do together, by phone or another messenger, so there is no helper for it. Ask your chief, or see the peer bridge reference.
